## Phonotactics
The language is generally (C)V(C), where C may in addition to true singular consonants stand for a stop followed by another consonant.

Rephrased any C must neighbor at least one V.

However most standard words (neither particle, pronoun, nor ending-derived) are CV(C). While Pronouns are VC and logic particles V.

When combining words, a conflict with this structure may arise. There are a few resolution strategies in order:
1. When a plosive is followed by a fricative with the same place, they combine into a matching ejective affricative.
2. When a plosive is followed by another consonant, it is articulated as a stop.
3. When a conflict is (partially) caused by a shortened word (eg. ram -> r), that word may be partially or fully unshortened.
4. Otherwise a shwa may be inserted.
